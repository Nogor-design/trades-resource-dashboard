The document now clearly says the system is built around:

active assignments tracked in spreadsheets
open orders tracked on paper and memory
recruiter workflow being hard to monitor
forecasting renewals, extensions, time off, and redeployment taking too much owner attention

That is exactly the right framing.

You are no longer saying, “Look at this cool dashboard.”

You are saying:

“This helps replace spreadsheet + paper + memory with one operational command center.”

That is the right business message.

2. The first-meeting demo flow is correct

You correctly narrowed the first meeting to four screens:

Owner Today View
Assignment Forecast
Open Orders Board
Recruiter Process Tracker

You also correctly moved Candidate Matching, Redeployment Planning, and Submittal Packets into future expansion territory.

What I would improve before the first demo
1. Make the first screen less crowded

The Owner Today View has sections A through H. That is powerful, but it may be too much for the first 60 seconds.

For the demo, I would make the first view show only four major blocks by default:

Needs Attention Today
Forecast Risk
Open Orders Needing Action
Owner Sales Focus

Then put the rest in expandable sections.

The owner should not have to process eight sections immediately. She should instantly see:

“Here is what I need to deal with today.”

2. Put “Owner Sales Focus” higher

Right now, Owner Sales Focus is section E. I would move it higher, right after Urgent Actions.

Why? Because that was her stated growth goal.

Recommended order:

Urgent Actions Today
Owner Sales Focus
Renewal & Extension Forecast
Upcoming Time Off
Assignments Ending Soon
Stuck Open Positions
Recruiter Workload Summary
Bench Depth Signals

This makes the demo feel more strategic.

3. Add three scripted demo stories

This will make the demo feel real instead of like a tour of screens.

Create three fake examples and walk through them.

Story 1: Assignment ending soon

“Jason is ending in 7 days. The system flags him as urgent, shows the extension status, shows whether the client has been contacted, and creates the next action.”

This demonstrates assignment forecasting.

Story 2: Open order stuck

“A CNC role has been open for 9 days with no submitted candidates. The system flags it as stuck and shows what action is needed.”

This demonstrates open order tracking.

Story 3: Recruiter process issue

“A candidate is almost ready to submit, but pay rate and availability are missing. The system flags the missing fields before the recruiter submits an incomplete profile.”

This demonstrates process consistency.

These stories will be more persuasive than showing every feature.

What I would not add yet

Do not add more AI features before the first demo.

Candidate Matching and Submittal Packets are good future expansion sections, and your document correctly labels them that way.

For the first meeting, too much AI could distract from the immediate operational value.

Also, do not over-focus on the technical launch instructions. The documentation includes streamlit run app.py, which is fine for you or a developer, but she does not need to care about that during the meeting.

Best first-demo sequence

Use this exact structure:

1. Start with the problem

Say:

“Based on your answers, I focused this demo around the things you said were hardest to track: assignment durations, extensions, time off, renewals, open orders, candidate approvals, recruiter process updates, and forecasting.”

2. Show Owner Today View

Say:

“This is the daily view. Instead of having to remember everything, this surfaces what needs attention today.”

Only show the top summary and urgent actions first.

3. Show Assignment Forecast

Say:

“This is the Gantt-style view you mentioned. The goal is to see what is ending, what needs renewal, who has time off, and where extension decisions are pending.”

4. Show Open Orders Board

Say:

“This replaces paper order tracking. It shows all open orders, who has been submitted, whether they were approved, and what action is needed next.”

5. Show Recruiter Process Tracker

Say:

“This helps make sure the process is followed consistently without turning it into micromanagement.”

6. Close with a question

Ask:

“Of these four areas, which would create the most value first if we turned the prototype into a real working system?”

That question is important because it moves the conversation toward a paid next step.
